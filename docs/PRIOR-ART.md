# Prior art in this hackathon, and what Ainek adds

Moved here from the README on Nov 10, 2026, with one pointer adjusted; two rows, marked "read Nov 10", were added the same day. The method and the full list of entries we looked at are in
[FIELD.md](FIELD.md) (in Russian).

We read the code of the nearest entries and looked at their contracts on chain on Nov 6, 2026. We did not run them or open their demos, and they may have moved on since. The two rows marked "read Nov 10" rest on posts, READMEs and parts of the code, checked against the repos, not on a full read. Rows marked (Tier 2) describe a part of Ainek that ran on testnet on Nov 7, through the Telegram bot except for a rejection and the stop button; see "Tier 2 flow" in the [README](../README.md#tier-2-flow).

| Project | What it does | Where it is ahead of Ainek | What Ainek adds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [TEE Receipts](https://github.com/tmdry4530/tee-receipts) | Receipts for NEAR AI answers; signer registry with key history, registered by the contract owner; on-chain signature check | Raw evidence in the repo; the contract checks the answer's signature itself | A hash-chained log designed for independent verifiers (our registration is owner-gated today too); the client refuses **before sending**, not after; E2EE to the attested key; salted commits |
| [Vouch](https://github.com/Oduwal/Vouch) | An AI posts a bond on its answer; disputes go to a staked jury | The deepest attestation check we saw (DCAP, RTMR0-3 replay, compose, NVIDIA signature, gateway), with raw evidence in transactions that anyone can re-check | A public key log; the check happens before a prompt leaves the browser; E2EE |
| [Charter](https://github.com/Spagero763/charter) | An AI fund with a mandate; an OutLayer guardian signs trades; on mainnet | Live on mainnet; the guardian's key derives from the wasm hash, so a new build gets a new key | The model route is verified against a public log and encrypted; (Tier 2) the policy stays private |
| [AgentGuard](https://github.com/CodeMasterLabs/agentguard) | The agent asks, an on-chain vault pays, holds or refuses; an optional TEE risk oracle | Deployed and in use, with MCP and Intents | (Tier 2) Every action needs a decision signed in the enclave, and the policy stays private; the model route is verified and encrypted |
| [Judge on NEAR](https://github.com/vijaygopalbalasa/judge-near) | A TDX judge and escrow between agents | The browser checks each run's quote and wasm hash; the contract has no keys | Private AI for people, not a verdict between agents; a public key log |
| [Black box](https://github.com/tenxagent/blackbox) | A hash-chained decision log of an agent, anchored on mainnet | The registry has no keys, so its immutability is provable | The log covers TEE keys and where an answer came from; (Tier 2) action proofs bound to a signed decision and a model proof |
| [Measure](https://github.com/dreyyyy1/measure) | Independently checks TDX quotes of NEAR's MPC nodes | A third party re-checks Intel's signature itself, without trusting the network | It covers MPC nodes and blocks nothing; we log AI model keys, and the client acts on the log |
| [PrivatePay Agent](https://github.com/Nassami1/privatepay-agent) | An invoice is redacted locally, then an agent pays through a contract with public caps | Deployed, with a large audience | The client verifies TDX and GPU evidence itself (its README lists quote verification as not implemented yet); E2EE instead of redaction; (Tier 2) a private policy |
| [Provable AI Compliance](https://github.com/zavodil/near-attested-ai) (read Nov 10: post, README, parts of the code) | A compliance gate for a company's AI requests: public policy packs in a NEAR registry, rules applied inside an OutLayer TDX enclave that then calls a NEAR AI model; a signed label per request | One Intel quote per run over the gateway's code, input, output and caller; the browser refuses to seal a prompt to an enclave whose attestation fails; label keys used only inside the TEE; the registry has no edit or delete method; one no-key command re-checks a recorded day (10 attested runs, 6 refusals, a forged label) | A public log of model keys written by verifiers, and E2EE to the model's attested key (theirs: TLS from the enclave to the model). A refusal when the model's evidence fails: theirs records the failed check and still delivers the answer, labelled `model_attested: true`. By default their rule questions are answered by a model outside any TEE (their README says so). (Tier 2) money moved by a contract on the decision |
| [CIPHERWORK](https://github.com/wadadawadada/cipherwork) (read Nov 10: post, README, parts of the code; two commits, both Nov 9) | Private inference with payment on proof: the client verifies TDX evidence (GPU evidence when present) with a fresh nonce, sends E2EE to the attested key, checks the signed reply; an escrow contract settles on a verifier's signed verdict | Refuses before sending when the evidence fails, and verifies every model attestation candidate returned; payment is tied to the proof on chain | A public key log written by verifiers (theirs: one verifier key of the project); a TCB policy that accepts `UpToDate` only by default (configurable; theirs accepts `OutOfDate` too); salted commits of proofs |
| Dozens of spending guards (Veto, Leash, Tollgate, Claw Guard, Approval Moment, …) | Limits, a whitelist and a function-call key; an injection gets blocked | Several run on mainnet or have stronger contracts, for example a contract that builds the transaction itself | We are not another guard: (Tier 2) the point is a verifiable route from model to policy to chain, built so that someone other than the vendor can check it |

Among the 26 hackathon entries whose code we read on Nov 6, 2026, none combines a public log of TEE keys, a refusal before sending and E2EE to the attested key. We do not claim this for entries we read only from their README, or for later ones. None of them has independent verifiers writing to a public log of AI model keys, and neither do we: both verifiers that write to our log are ours. On Nov 10 we also read two later entries (posts, READMEs, parts of the code; the two rows marked so above). CIPHERWORK refuses before sending and encrypts to the attested model key, with no public key log: it trusts one verifier key of its own. Provable AI Compliance refuses and encrypts at another layer, to an attested OutLayer enclave that then reaches the model over TLS; it has no key log, and when the model's evidence fails a check it records that and still delivers the answer. It binds its gateway's code, input, output and caller into one Intel quote per run, where our Tier 2 has two artifacts joined by hashes.
