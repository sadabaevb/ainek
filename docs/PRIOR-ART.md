# Prior art in this hackathon, and what Ainek adds

We read the code of the nearest entries and looked at their contracts on chain on Nov 6, 2026. We did not run them or open their demos, and they may have moved on since. The two rows marked "read Nov 10" rest on posts, READMEs and parts of the code, checked against the repos, not on a full read. The five rows marked "read Nov 13" name what was read, all of it with Claude Code: Countersign directly, the other four by delegated agents, and the statements in those four rows were then checked against the source a second time. Rows marked (Tier 2) describe a part of Ainek that ran on testnet on Nov 7, through the Telegram bot except for a rejection and the stop button; see "Tier 2 flow" in the [README](../README.md#tier-2-flow).

Among the 26 hackathon entries whose code we read on Nov 6, 2026, none combines a public log of TEE keys, a refusal before sending and E2EE to the attested key. We do not claim this for entries we read only from their README, or for later ones. None of them has independent verifiers writing to a public log of AI model keys, and neither do we: both verifiers that write to our log are ours. On Nov 10 we also read two later entries (posts, READMEs, parts of the code; the two rows marked so above). CIPHERWORK refuses before sending and encrypts to the attested model key, with no public key log: it trusts one verifier key of its own. Provable AI Compliance refuses and encrypts at another layer, to an attested OutLayer enclave that then reaches the model over TLS; it has no key log, and when the model's evidence fails a check it records that and still delivers the answer. It binds its gateway's code, input, output and caller into one Intel quote per run, where our Tier 2 has two artifacts joined by hashes.

On Nov 13 the gallery held 820 entries. We read the text of 779 of their posts, searched GitHub for code that reads NEAR AI's evidence, and read 23 more repositories with Claude Code (Countersign directly, 22 by delegated agents under one brief; what is said here about the nearest was then checked against the source a second time). The sentence about the 26 does not describe this later field. **Countersign** keeps a signed, hash-chained feed of reviewed NEAR AI machines in a NEAR contract and refuses before sending; its reviewer is its author, as our verifiers are ours. It is ahead of us on the contract (no access keys; ours has one), on boot measurements recomputed from the OS image and on published evidence. By its own method page it does not check response signatures, follow an image to its source or compare the release per request, and its encryption is version 2. **Shade** has a key registry that watchers fill by a quorum the contract enforces, a refusal before sending and receipts on chain; by its README the gateway in its demo is a mock and nothing is on testnet. **Neslos** follows the enclave's action log to the public compose file and to image provenance, as we do, and pins one reviewed state in a file. **DNA Confidential** runs the same client path as ours in the browser, with no key log and no contract. In none of the 51 repositories read so far did we find three things: an append-only, hash-chained key log for several verifiers with the trust set and the quorum at the viewer; release marks in such a log with a client rule on them; a policy kept private whose decision is signed inside an OutLayer job and verified by the contract (Charter signs in OutLayer under a public mandate; Neslos signs in an enclave of its own, and its vault verifies the signature). That is a statement about 51 repositories, not about 820 entries. The method, the numbers and the other entries of that reading are in [FIELD.md](FIELD.md).
